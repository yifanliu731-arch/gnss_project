# Numerical workflow

SeaArrayCalib operation guides: [中文](user-guide.zh-CN.md) · [English](user-guide.en.md). This document describes the numerical assumptions and limits behind the controls.

## Survey simulation

The initial array has four corner beacons spanning 6 km east–west and 6 km north–south, centred at the ENU origin: E/N = ±3000 m, with depths B1 = 3150 m, B2 = 3180 m, B3 = 3220 m and B4 = 3250 m (negative U; mean depth 3200 m). Reset restores these coordinates. Users can change all coordinates and the beacon count.

- Enter 1–64 beacon ENU coordinate triples. This is an input limit, not a claim that every layout is identifiable.
- Six complete track families use `vendor/seaarray_core/research/src/risk_design/closed_tours.py`: circle, figure eight, clover, square, four-petal rose and circle plus cross. The first five retain closed curves. Circle plus cross follows one CCW circle from E, the E–W diameter, W–N diagonal transfer and N–S diameter before rotation. Its complete length is `(2π + 4 + √2)r`; the transfer length `√2r` is included in sailing time. It ends at S without a forced return. Transfer sampling is off by default (`collect_transfer=false`); only full acquisition slots outside the transfer are retained. UTC-like elapsed times and round-robin slot IDs are not compressed or renumbered when transfer slots are skipped. Transfer sampling can be enabled for a controlled comparison. Route order is fixed; angle rotates the route.
- Scale limits are user inputs, expressed as maximum radial extent divided by the current mean beacon depth (default 0.5–10, supported 0.01–100). Square side length is also exported. Seed scales are supplemented by all in-range complete-route budget boundaries calculated from current speed, depth and budgets, then 0–3 user-selected integer rounds (default 1), or target-time-gap stopping, of log-midpoint refinement around C1, C2 and C3 winners at risk radius 1 per family/angle/budget. All existing candidates remain in the pool after each round. Every round chooses fresh winners from the enlarged pool; an unchanged winner does not stop refinement early. Fixed-round mode stops at the requested count. Target-time-gap mode stops when every currently proposed winner-neighbour interval is no wider than the requested complete-route duration gap. The existing 2000-candidate cap raises an explicit error rather than dropping candidates. The final pool is shared by all three criteria and all budgets. It is a finite refined search, not a global optimum. No radius exceeds the supplied bounds to fill time.
- Routes use the current horizontal array centroid. Circle angles are starting phases; other angles rotate the shape. Candidate IDs, scale sources, refinement round, actual scale, closure semantics and input snapshot are exported. Over-budget and unsupported routes retain reasons. New search/ray/support calculations run when inputs change; saved results remain labelled with their original configuration.
- Serial polling addresses one beacon per slot. Slots use the larger of the requested interval and conservative two-way acoustic occupancy, with a one-second reply allowance. The vessel is level, with RFU lever arm rotated by route heading. Observations are single-way equivalent; transmit/receive movement within a ping is not separately modelled.
- Slant-range coverage, ray support and random dropouts mask observations. Each beacon needs at least eight valid observations. Candidates exceeding 12,000 slots are rejected explicitly; no silent truncation.
- Uses layered Snell rays. Perturbations are integrated along curved reference rays with a declared `exp(-depth/1000 m)` vertical shape, shared OU amplitude and fixed horizontal gradients. Default SSP: Munk.
- Candidates share an OU realisation on the union of observation times. Seeded acoustic noise, GNSS noise and dropout streams are candidate-specific and insensitive to candidate order. Changing the candidate set can change the shared field realisation; changing only the risk radius does not change that pool or realisation.
- All beacon coordinates enter **one joint fit**, with common sound-speed offset, common delay, linear drift and N−1 relative beacon delays: **4N+2 parameters**. No coordinate or true-depth priors. Initial coordinates are displaced from simulation truth. Nuisance prior SDs: 2 m/s, 5 ms, 1 ms/hour and 5 ms. Checks cover the remaining scaled Gauss–Newton correction and augmented-design rank.
- C1 is the mean coordinate covariance diagonal under the nuisance-prior model. C3 adds the largest coordinate bias-covariance eigenvalue divided by 3N, multiplied by the squared user risk radius (default 1). OU covariance and gradient magnitudes define that residual set. Scoring approximates independent GNSS errors as timing variance. Effective timing SD has a 0.1 microsecond numerical floor, including zero-noise tests.
- Plan A minimises theoretical risk among eligible candidates. Plan B minimises time within the lower half of eligible risks. Plan C equally weights min–max normalised risk and duration. These are finite-candidate choices. Single-run coordinate errors and fitted residuals are separate outputs, not repeated-trial MSE or proof of theoretical ranking.
- Export selected-plan observations as CSV; configuration, scores, failures, estimates, model notes and source hashes as JSON. Units are seconds and metres. Range is an assumed cutoff; the simulator does not infer device range or precision from frequency.

## Refinement stopping modes

The dropdown lists 0, 1, 2 and 3 rounds, then **Refine to target time gap**. API `refinement_mode` is `rounds` by default, or `time_gap`. Fixed-round requests use `refinement_rounds` (default 1; legacy API counts through 10 remain accepted). Target mode uses `refinement_time_gap_min`, default **5 minutes**, supporting 0.1–2880 minutes; a supplied fixed-round count is inactive in target mode and omitted from the effective saved settings.

For each family, angle, budget and unit-radius criterion, take the current eligible winner and its adjacent sizes in the complete sorted candidate list. Check the difference between their **full sailing durations**, including transfers, in minutes. Bisect only intervals wider than the target, using the existing geometric-mean midpoint. Invalid designs can remain interval boundaries but cannot win; missing neighbours at a size boundary are not invented. Re-evaluate winners and intervals after every round. This resolves local time spacing near promising sizes, not the entire scale grid, objective convergence or continuous global optimality. Changing the ship speed changes this duration spacing.

Results and ZIP manifests record the mode, requested time gap, nonempty refinement rounds completed, largest remaining winner-neighbour gap, `target_met` and `stop_reason`. An empty eligible neighbourhood reports `no_refinement_intervals` rather than claiming the target was reached. The 2000-candidate cap remains an error. A 30-round safety guard also fails explicitly if intervals still exceed the target; it never silently returns a supposedly completed target. Search and stopping use design scores before validation, and retain the unit-radius pool shared by all chosen risk radii.

## Time budget comparison

Enter a minimum and maximum budget in hours (0.1–48, minimum strictly below maximum). The server generates ten values equally spaced in log time, including both endpoints: `exp(log(min) + i*(log(max)-log(min))/9)`, i=0…9. The UI shows the values before running and a selectable result row for every budget afterward. Defaults are 5–25 h; the ten values are calculated from the inputs, not a fixed list.

Candidates are generated and scored up to the largest cap. Each of the ten caps selects Plans A/B/C from design-feasible candidates before any validation fit. Munk mode then fits every design-feasible candidate once, preserving the same observations, noise and complete-route cost. A failed fit keeps its frozen recommendation and failure reason; no successful alternative is substituted. `valid` and `n_valid` describe design feasibility in both modes. `validation_status` describes the separate fit outcome; Munk results also include per-budget and overall success/failure counts. The Munk selection hash covers the saved `search.frozen_selection` (sorted-key JSON), independently of validation outcomes. Budgets with no eligible route are retained explicitly. Selecting a budget updates the route, plans, solution and observations export. Run JSON contains all ten budgets, choices and unique selected plans with observations. Older API clients using only `time_budget_h` retain a single-cap result. The existing usable slant-range input remains independent and adjustable.

## Field-data mode

Upload GNSS, acoustic delays and a sound-speed profile; specify beacon IDs and the RFU lever arm. The field page defaults to joint calibration: N free 3D beacon coordinates, shared sound-speed offset, common effective delay, linear delay drift and N−1 relative delays (4N+2 parameters). B5 is the relative-delay reference when selected, otherwise the lowest selected ID. The mean model, analytic derivatives and nuisance priors are shared with joint simulation and match the SeaArrayCalib field model. Single-beacon mode invokes the same estimator independently for each beacon (six parameters per fit); legacy solvers remain selectable. Blank IDs select all IDs detected in the travel-time file. Valid UTC alignment is required; joint modes never substitute index alignment or GNSS extrapolation. Uploaded observations are not automatically clipped by fitted residuals. Reported standard deviations are residual-scaled conditional internal precision, not coordinate truth errors. A deeper supplied profile and original quality control should be considered when interpreting a result. GNSS antenna ENU plus the attitude-rotated antenna-to-transducer RFU displacement supplies transducer positions. The profile is preserved at full resolution, and the compiled ray integrals and analytic derivatives are reused during iteration.

Uploaded sound-speed values are used without smoothing or decimation. Below the deepest supplied point, the current field ray tracer uses a declared linear extension of 0.016 m/s per metre. For the bundled 0–3242 m profile, the internal extension reaches 6000 m. These added values are a model assumption, not measured samples. The displayed uploaded profile remains unchanged. Supply a deeper measured profile when appropriate.

## Criteria and interpretation

C1 is the nominal coordinate covariance trace per coordinate after nuisance elimination. With the scaled coordinate gain `Q = Gx / sqrt(3N)` and declared environmental second moment `K`, define `M = Q K Qᵀ`. **C2 = C1 + ρ² tr(M)** is the average-risk score under that second-moment model. **C3 = C1 + ρ² λmax(M)** is the worst-direction score for the corresponding ellipsoid of radius ρ. For the same matrix C2 ≥ C3, but their underlying distribution and bounded-set interpretations differ: this numerical inequality does not rank the robustness of the selected routes. The mean offset is retained by using an uncentered second moment. C1 averages over centered nuisance priors; it need not equal the fixed-nuisance Monte Carlo noise MSE.

Neither environmental penalty is automatically inferred from post-fit residuals. Scores are local model quantities, separate from held-out coordinate errors. A score is not a universal error bound, a confidence envelope, or evidence of independent field accuracy. Search includes neighbourhoods of all three winners evaluated at radius 1 using the same refinement rule. The exported unit_c2_m2 and unit_c3_m2 retain those reference scores. Changing the selected criterion or risk radius does not change the candidate pool for otherwise identical settings. Radius-dependent recommendations use the actual C2/C3 scores on that common finite pool; the search does not claim a continuous optimum at each radius.

The independent input `risk_radius` (ρ) defaults to 1 and accepts finite values from 0 to 100. It primarily tunes Munk design perturbation strength and also applies to uploaded-profile risk. Keep ρ = 1 for the scale derived from supplied training profiles. It scales design amplitude, so environmental penalties scale as ρ²: at ρ = 0, C2 and C3 equal C1 with the same candidates and tie rules. It does not change the physical validation field, noise, dropout, nominal precision or estimator. Values above 1 enlarge the declared design set; they do not specify an error ceiling or confidence probability. Saved settings and run results always record the effective radius, including defaulted API requests.

## Profile-driven simulation

Select `sound_field_mode=profiles` and supply `sound_field.background_csv` (`depth_m,sound_speed_mps`) and `sound_field.history_csv` (`date,depth_m,sound_speed_mps`). Dates use YYYY-MM-DD. Negative depth, nonpositive speed, missing values, duplicate date/depth pairs and unsupported coverage are rejected. Profiles must start at zero and cover the full common depth interval, reaching at least 100 m below the deepest beacon. A piecewise-linear common grid retains every input knot. There is no automatic smoothing, decimation or deep extrapolation. A prepared deep extension, if needed, must be supplied and documented by the user. Per file limit is 16 MiB; supported limits are 1500 dates, 6000 unique knots and 2 million grid values. These are explicit software limits, not a physical data-quality standard.

`training_end` is inclusive. `validation_start` must be later and is inclusive; intermediate dates are an unused gap. All history minus background differences are formed on the common grid. Only training differences enter the uncentered SVD, retaining singular values above 1e-10 times the largest. The factor satisfies `BBᵀ = Dtrainᵀ Dtrain / ntrain` to numerical precision. The reference-ray knot sensitivity `H` uses eight-point quadrature per layer; `M = (Q H B)(Q H B)ᵀ`. The separate profile multiplier `a` scales both declared risk (`a² M`) and test anomalies (`a × delta`). With risk radius ρ, the C2/C3 environmental penalties use `a² ρ² M`, while test anomalies remain `a × delta`. Both controls default to 1; keeping both at 1 retains the supplied scale. Available factors are 0.5, 1, 2 and 5. They are sensitivity settings, not occurrence probabilities or spatial gradients. The unit second-moment ellipsoid is not calibrated to contain all training or validation profiles.

Candidate scores, refinements, GDOP checks and all budget selections are frozen using the fixed background and training data. They do not use validation error, test-ray availability or fitted residuals. Each selected C1/C2/C3 route is tested on one specified validation date or every validation date; identical route/date jobs are reused. A/B/C display alternatives are additionally fitted on the detail date. Unselected candidates keep their design scores and are labelled accordingly. Failed selected routes remain selected and retain failure reasons. Munk mode also freezes choices before validation, but fits all design-feasible candidates rather than only selected plans. Both modes use the same A/B/C selector with candidate-ID tie breaking.

For each date, truth is the fixed background plus the scaled historical anomaly, static over the complete tour. Layered rays are retraced through this truth profile. The nominal inversion still uses the fixed background with the same 4N+2 estimator, priors and convergence criteria. Existing accepted fast roots are retained; a bracketed fallback recovers failed numerical roots only inside the physical monotone Snell branch, accepting range closure ≤10 μm (final closure gate ≤100 μm). Turning/reflected paths are not introduced. Unreachable observations and acoustic occupancy violations are masked without compressing time, replacing delays by straight rays, shortening the tour or redrawing noise. At least eight observations per beacon, retained GDOP and estimation checks remain mandatory.

All compared routes receive the same daily profile. White and navigation noise have deterministic route/calendar seeds; a route keeps the same draw across amplitude changes, generated before the truth mask. MSE is the mean of squared errors over 3N coordinate components. 3D RMS equals `sqrt(3 MSE)`. Per-route means over successful dates are explicitly conditional when failures occur. The C1–C3 paired mean additionally uses only dates where both succeeded. No significance claim is generated; adjacent daily profiles need not be independent.

`field_validation` records the split, input hashes, risk rank, frozen choices, per-date errors, missing rays and failures. The saved configuration retains both CSV texts. Detail-date observations distinguish `delay_s` (noisy truth), `true_delay_s` (exact perturbed ray), `nominal_delay_s` (background ray) and `field_delay_s` (their difference). Residuals remain observed minus the complete fitted prediction. Original slot IDs are retained. Selection and scores are never recomputed from the retained test observations.

The bundled [South China Sea example](../examples/south_china_sea_profiles/README.md) is background-aligned daily reanalysis with an explicit taper and deep-extension assumption. Its 182/14/170 calendar split is retrospective. The software supports the workflow, not a claim that a new quick run reproduces a complete controlled experiment.
